## Scope rulings from review

- Linux/AMD64/ARM64/AppArmor operation remains a mandatory exact-head CI gate;
  local macOS evidence cannot certify it. Platform failures block adoption.
- Unsupported hard CPU/memory/process-count/detached-session controls refuse
  admission. Consumers requiring them need another qualified boundary.
- Exact timeout/output subtypes remain toolEffectUncertain; richer diagnostics
  require upstream work. This reaffirms the initial evidence ruling.
- General hostile-code/DoS and trusted dependency/callback compromise are not
  certified. Broader deployment needs additional isolation qualification.
- Require frozen owned files and drained writers. Local checksums detect
  corruption; they provide neither race elimination nor hostile-host integrity.
- Consumers authenticate operators and design their approval UI/policy;
  identifiers alone cannot authorize production work.
- Permission covers one bounded invocation, not each effect or retries/refunds.
  Consequential effects require domain approval and reconciliation.
- Reuse the local registry and drained-writer inspection; existing lock metadata
  is not renewed consent. Distributed consumers need fencing/storage guarantees.
- The synthetic loopback server is a fixture; it must not be deployed publicly.
- No new live efficacy, tuning or memory/correction benefit is established.
  Consumers still need representative model evaluations.
- Retain service/accounting proof and requalify new package payloads. Local
  canaries do not replace the later database/platform acceptance matrix.
